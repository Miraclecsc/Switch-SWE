Also, Higher resolution makes hub-spreading **more** attractive under optimization, not less. Sketch: a node `v`'s contribution to `sum_C (k_tilde_C / 2m)^2`, summed over the `O_v` communities containing it, is which *decreases* in `O_v`. So increasing `O_v` saves null-model penalty, and resolution scales the size of those savings. Quick check on a star (hub `h`, 4 leaves, `m = 4`): One community `{h, l1, l2, l3, l4}`: `EQ = 1 γ` Two communities `{h, l1, l2}, {h, l3, l4}`, `O_h = 2`: `EQ = 1/2 γ/2` Difference `(1 γ)/2`: at resolution < 1 the non-overlapping cover wins, at resolution > 1 the spread cover wins. Intuition: higher resolution -> bigger absolute null-model penalty -> bigger savings from anything that shrinks `k_tilde_C`, and spreading high-degree nodes does exactly that. None of this affects the current API or docstring (the cover is input, not optimized).
